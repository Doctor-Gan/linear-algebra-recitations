## Clarifications in the exposition

- With circle equation `x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0`, the coefficient system is `M(D,E,F)^T = -q`. The handwritten sketch leaves this matrix equation abbreviated. The minus sign is explicit in the chapter.
- The positivity of the squared radius is proved from the existence of distinct real points satisfying the equation.
- The circle discussion distinguishes the number of coefficient solutions from the number of points on a single circle. It uses consistency of the coefficient system as the concyclicity criterion, without a determinant digression.
- All operations in the parameter calculation are specified. Division by `1 - lambda` occurs only after the case `lambda = 1` has been separated.
- The sentence about quintics means that there is no general formula by radicals. It does not claim that every quintic is unsolvable by radicals.
- The kernel is defined before it is used. For rectangular matrices, trivial kernel is equivalent to full column rank, but does not guarantee consistency for every right-hand side. The square hypothesis is identified precisely in the proof of universal solvability.
- The broad mathematical introduction is expanded from the preparation discussion, including the limit example in analysis and the distinction between great-circle routes and their map projections.
